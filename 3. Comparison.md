
# Ghost Queue — Solution Comparison

## 1. Purpose

This document compares alternative approaches to identifying unresolved administrative cases and explains why a hybrid, evidence-based workflow auditor is a reasonable proposed solution.

The comparison is qualitative. It does not represent measured benchmark results.

## 2. Approaches Compared

### Approach A: Manual Monitoring

Staff periodically inspect pending cases and follow up using existing tools.

**Advantages**
- Simple to introduce
- Human judgment remains central
- Minimal technical complexity

**Limitations**
- Repetitive and time-consuming
- Difficult to scale across large queues
- Relies on consistent manual follow-up
- May miss patterns across departments

### Approach B: Fixed-Rule Alerts

A conventional system flags cases based on rules such as elapsed time, missing fields, or transfer counts.

**Advantages**
- Transparent and explainable
- Predictable behavior
- Straightforward to test
- No language model required for detection

**Limitations**
- Thresholds require configuration and maintenance
- May generate false positives if workflow context is ignored
- Limited ability to interpret unstructured notes
- Rule coverage depends on workflow definitions

### Approach C: AI-Only Assessment

A language model reviews case information and independently produces a risk assessment or recommendation.

**Advantages**
- Can summarize unstructured text
- Can produce readable explanations
- Flexible when records contain varied language

**Limitations**
- May produce unsupported conclusions
- Scores may be inconsistent
- Decisions can be difficult to reproduce
- May introduce additional cost and latency
- Requires privacy and access safeguards

### Approach D: Ghost Queue Hybrid Approach

Deterministic rules identify measurable warning signs, while AI assists with evidence-grounded explanations. A transparent prioritization policy supports human review.

**Advantages**
- Measurable conditions are detected using testable rules
- AI adds readable summaries where useful
- Recommendations can be linked to recorded evidence
- Human reviewers retain decision-making authority
- Components can be tested independently

**Limitations**
- Depends on accurate and sufficiently complete records
- Requires threshold configuration and validation
- AI summaries need safeguards against unsupported claims
- Integration and monitoring introduce engineering work

## 3. Qualitative Comparison

| Criterion | Manual | Fixed Rules | AI-Only | Ghost Queue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Transparency | High | High | Variable | High if evidence-linked |
| Scalability | Limited | Good | Depends on cost and design | Depends on implementation |
| Repeatability | Variable | High | Variable | High for rule-based detection |
| Unstructured-text support | Human-dependent | Limited | Strong potential | AI-assisted |
| False-positive control | Human-dependent | Threshold-dependent | Requires validation | Requires validation |
| Human oversight | Direct | Configurable | Must be designed | Core requirement |
| Implementation effort | Low initially | Moderate | Moderate to high | Moderate to high |

This comparison reflects design-level expectations, not results from a controlled experiment.

## 4. Why the Hybrid Approach?

Ghost Queue avoids relying exclusively on a language model for measurable detection.

A rule can establish that a case has been inactive for a defined period or transferred a specified number of times. AI can then help explain these findings in accessible language without inventing facts or replacing the underlying checks.

This separation improves testability: detection rules can be evaluated against labelled examples, and generated explanations can be checked for factual consistency.

## 5. Feasibility and Trade-Offs

A limited prototype can begin with structured sample records, deterministic rules, a database, and a simple review interface. AI-generated summaries can be added as a separate component.

A realistic pilot should measure precision, recall, false-positive rates, explanation accuracy, and the effect of human intervention.

Synthetic data is useful for testing workflow behavior but cannot establish real-world effectiveness.

## 6. Final Recommendation

The proposed Ghost Queue hybrid approach is a balanced candidate because it combines measurable detection, readable explanations, transparent prioritization, and human accountability.

It should be selected for a controlled prototype and validated before any claim of operational effectiveness is made.
