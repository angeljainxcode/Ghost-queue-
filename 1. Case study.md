
# Ghost Queue — Case Study
## Find the People a System Has Forgotten

### 1. Executive Summary

Administrative workflows can appear efficient when measured by completed tasks while some cases remain unresolved for long periods. Cases may become stuck because information is missing, responsibility is unclear, departments repeatedly transfer them, or follow-up does not occur.

**Ghost Queue** is a proposed AI-assisted workflow auditing system that identifies potentially stalled cases, explains observable warning signs, and helps human operators prioritize follow-up actions.

Its objective is to make overlooked cases visible and support timely, evidence-based intervention without replacing human decision-making.

### 2. Problem Statement

Traditional workflow monitoring often focuses on aggregate indicators such as completed tasks, average processing time, and total pending cases. These indicators may fail to reveal individual cases experiencing repeated delays or handoffs.

A case can remain technically active while making no meaningful progress. Without case-level monitoring, these cases may receive insufficient attention.

Ghost Queue focuses on three primary warning signals:

- **Stagnation:** A case has remained without meaningful progress beyond an applicable threshold.
- **Repeated handoffs:** A case has moved between departments or responsible teams multiple times.
- **Missing information:** A case cannot progress because required information or documentation is unavailable.

These signals indicate cases requiring review. They do not automatically prove negligence or administrative failure.

### 3. Stakeholders

- **Applicants and service users:** Need timely handling and clear updates.
- **Caseworkers:** Need visibility into pending work and missing requirements.
- **Department supervisors:** Need to identify bottlenecks and coordinate ownership.
- **Auditors and administrators:** Need traceable evidence for reviewing workflow performance.

### 4. Root-Cause Hypotheses

The following are hypotheses to investigate, not established findings about a specific institution:

1. Responsibility may become unclear after a case is transferred.
2. Required information may be missing or inconsistently recorded.
3. Cases may remain pending without a defined follow-up mechanism.
4. Aggregate metrics may hide individual cases experiencing prolonged delays.
5. Manual monitoring may become difficult as case volume increases.

### 5. Investigation Method

A responsible investigation should examine authorized case-level records containing, where available:

- Case identifier and creation date
- Current status and last meaningful activity
- Assigned department or owner
- Transfer and handoff history
- Required information and completion status
- Applicable deadlines
- Recorded actions and resolution dates

The analysis must distinguish verified facts from assumptions. Synthetic or illustrative records must be labelled clearly and must not be presented as real institutional evidence.

### 6. Key Investigation Questions

1. Which cases have exceeded their applicable processing thresholds?
2. Which cases have experienced repeated transfers without resolution?
3. Which cases are blocked by identifiable missing information?
4. Can every warning be traced to specific recorded evidence?
5. Which cases should be reviewed first, and why?
6. How can false alarms and unfair prioritization be reduced?

### 7. Scope and Limitations

Ghost Queue is designed as a decision-support concept for structured administrative workflows. Its reliability depends on data quality, appropriate thresholds, and human review.

It cannot reliably infer undocumented events, establish intent from delays alone, or guarantee that a flagged case has been neglected. A pilot should validate its detection rules against human-reviewed cases before operational deployment.

### 8. Conclusion

The central problem is not simply the existence of pending work. It is the possibility that unresolved cases remain invisible within aggregate workflow statistics.

Ghost Queue proposes case-level anomaly detection, evidence-linked explanations, and human-reviewed intervention prioritization.

**Core principle:** Detect warning signs, explain the evidence, and support human action—without treating an algorithmic flag as a verdict.
