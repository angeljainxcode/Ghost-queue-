
# Ghost Queue — Expected Solutions

## 1. Objective

Develop a feasible workflow auditing system that identifies potentially stalled administrative cases, explains recorded warning signs, and recommends which cases should receive human attention first.

## 2. Proposed Solution Components

### A. Case Data Validation

Validate incoming records for missing fields, inconsistent statuses, invalid dates, and incomplete ownership information.

**Expected outcome:** More reliable inputs and explicit identification of data-quality problems.

### B. Stagnation Detection

Calculate the time since the last meaningful activity and compare it against configurable thresholds based on case type, priority, and applicable service-level requirements.

**Expected outcome:** Identify cases that may be waiting longer than expected.

### C. Repeated-Handoff Detection

Count department transfers and identify repeated routing patterns, particularly where transfers occur without recorded resolution or meaningful progress.

**Expected outcome:** Highlight cases that may be caught in a cycle of reassignment.

### D. Missing-Information Detection

Compare available records against the required fields or documents for each workflow stage.

**Expected outcome:** Identify potential blockers and specify which information appears to be missing.

### E. Evidence-Based AI Explanation

Use deterministic rules to detect measurable conditions. An AI language model may summarize those conditions in clear language, provided its explanation is grounded in recorded evidence.

**Expected outcome:** Reviewers can understand why a case was flagged instead of receiving an unexplained score.

### F. Intervention Prioritization

Rank flagged cases using a transparent policy that considers:

- Urgency and applicable deadlines
- Potential impact on the affected person
- Duration and severity of the blockage
- Confidence in the underlying evidence
- Estimated effort required for follow-up

The ranking policy must be configurable and reviewed by the responsible organization. Missing data should reduce confidence rather than automatically increase risk.

### G. Human Review and Audit Trail

Allow an authorized reviewer to examine the evidence, accept or dismiss a warning, record a reason, and choose the next action.

**Expected outcome:** Traceable decisions and a clear distinction between automated recommendations and human-approved actions.

## 3. End-to-End Workflow

1. Import authorized case records.
2. Validate and normalize the data.
3. Apply rules for stagnation, repeated handoffs, and missing information.
4. Generate evidence-linked explanations for flagged cases.
5. Calculate a transparent review priority.
6. Present cases to an authorized human reviewer.
7. Record the review outcome and chosen action.
8. Measure whether the intervention leads to meaningful progress.

## 4. Expected Outputs

For each flagged case, the system should aim to provide:

- Case identifier
- Warning category
- Relevant timestamps and transfer counts
- Missing fields or unresolved requirements
- Explanation grounded in available records
- Priority and confidence, with their calculation disclosed
- Suggested next step
- Human-review status and audit history

These are proposed outputs. They should be labelled as implemented only after they have been built and tested.

## 5. Evaluation Metrics

| Metric | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Precision | Proportion of flagged cases confirmed as needing review |
| Recall | Proportion of known problematic cases detected |
| False-positive rate | Frequency of incorrectly flagged acceptable cases |
| Detection latency | Time taken to identify qualifying cases |
| Explanation traceability | Whether reviewers can verify the evidence behind a warning |
| Intervention effectiveness | Whether reviewed cases make meaningful progress after action |

A pilot should establish baseline measurements and evaluate the system against human-reviewed cases.

## 6. Responsible AI Safeguards

- Do not automatically reject applications, close cases, or penalize staff.
- Restrict access to authorized users.
- Minimize personal data and protect sensitive records.
- Keep explanations linked to source evidence.
- Allow reviewers to correct inaccurate flags.
- Monitor performance across relevant case categories.
- Disclose uncertainty and missing data.
- Preserve an auditable record of recommendations and human decisions.

## 7. Expected Impact

If validated in a suitable pilot, Ghost Queue could help organizations identify delayed cases earlier, reduce avoidable handoffs, improve follow-up, and make workflow bottlenecks more visible.

These are expected benefits, not measured results. Their actual magnitude must be established through testing.
